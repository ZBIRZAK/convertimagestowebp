What is WebP?

A plain‑English explanation of the image format built for speed.

WebP is a modern image format that delivers smaller file sizes than JPG and PNG while keeping visual quality high. For e‑commerce, that means faster product pages, lower bandwidth costs, and more shoppers seeing crisp images without waiting. If you are still serving product photos as JPG or PNG, WebP is the single easiest upgrade you can make.

How WebP works

WebP uses advanced compression methods that remove data the human eye is unlikely to notice. In lossy mode, WebP behaves like an optimized JPG but usually produces a smaller file at the same perceived quality. In lossless mode, WebP keeps every pixel intact, similar to PNG, but still compresses more efficiently.

WebP vs JPG and PNG in simple terms

JPG is great for photos but it is older and less efficient. PNG is great for transparency and sharp graphics but files are large. WebP can do both: it supports transparency like PNG and smaller file sizes like JPG. That combination makes it ideal for product galleries where you need speed without sacrificing detail.

Why e‑commerce stores benefit most

Product pages are image‑heavy. A single product can include 5-10 photos, plus thumbnails across category pages. If each photo is heavy, the total page weight grows fast. WebP reduces each file, which lowers your Largest Contentful Paint (LCP) time and improves Core Web Vitals. Faster pages typically lead to better conversion rates.

When should you not use WebP?

There are only a few cases where WebP is not the best choice. If a marketplace or legacy integration requires JPG, keep JPG as a fallback. If your logo or UI icons require pixel‑perfect edges and transparency, use PNG or SVG. For most product photos and banners, WebP is a safe default.

What quality should you use?

A good starting point is 80-85 quality for product photos. Shiny or detailed products might need 85-90. For flat or simple imagery you can drop to 75 and still look sharp. The right quality is whatever keeps your product detail intact while delivering a meaningful size drop. Use the Image Reducer tool to test sizes quickly.

Recommended workflow for store owners

Export your product images at a consistent width (usually 1600-2000px for main product images). Convert to WebP in bulk using the Bulk WebP tool. Upload WebP to your store, regenerate thumbnails, and validate that all sizes are served in WebP. Use the PageSpeed Checker tool to confirm file size and dimensions are within target.

Common mistakes to avoid

Do not upload oversized originals and expect compression to fix everything. Do not keep mixed formats (JPG and WebP) inside the same gallery. Do not lazy‑load your hero product image because it hurts LCP. Instead, load the hero image eagerly and lazy‑load below‑the‑fold images.

Quick checklist

Use WebP for every product photo. Keep main images under 350 KB. Keep thumbnails under 80 KB. Set width and height attributes to prevent layout shift. Validate your top 10 products in PageSpeed Insights after the change.

FAQ

Is WebP supported everywhere? Yes, modern browsers support it. For older systems, keep a JPG fallback if needed.

Does WebP improve SEO? Indirectly, yes. Smaller files improve speed and Core Web Vitals, which help rankings.

Can WebP hurt quality? Only if quality is set too low. Use 80-90 for product photos and test visually.

Extra benefits store owners notice

Smaller images do not only help speed, they reduce bandwidth and hosting costs. If your store gets thousands of visits a day, shaving 200 KB from each product page can save gigabytes of transfer per week. That savings becomes real money on managed hosting plans or when you scale ad traffic.

WebP also makes your image pipeline more predictable. When you set a quality range and a consistent width, the file size is easier to control. That means fewer surprises like a single oversized product image slowing down the entire page.

How to test WebP quality the right way

Always compare the WebP version and the original side by side at 100% zoom. Look at texture, edges, and small text on packaging. If the image looks soft, increase quality by 5 points. If it looks identical, lower quality slightly and test again. This quick loop helps you find the smallest file size that still sells.

A simple migration plan

Start with your top 10 products, convert and upload, then measure speed. If results are good, move to the next 50 products. This phased approach avoids risk and lets you validate quality before committing to the full catalog.
